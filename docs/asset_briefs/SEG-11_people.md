# Segment 11: people (written 2026-09-18 by Claude Code)

The seven faction species of the catalog (`people`: human, elf, dwarf, gnome, goblin, orc, automaton), one brief per species and gender: 13 briefs, each describing the adult tier-0 south stand frame in numbers, then the four clothing tiers as layer changes, the five age stages as silhouette rules, and the AR-600 sheet the frame belongs to. Today every one of them is drawn from a shared stand-in sheet with a per-species tint (`people.<species>.tint`); these briefs replace all of that with original bodies. Request rows: AR-400 (people), AR-501 (clothing tiers), AR-601 (age stages), AR-600 (the sheet standard).

## How to work this segment
1. Style: high-resolution 2.5D in the manner of the reference squares, every frame inside one 48×48 screen-pixel square at native resolution (1 art pixel = 1 screen pixel at zoom 1). No 16×16 upscaling. `docs/ART_STANDARD.md` is binding.
2. Projection: a person stands on its feet in the bottom-right region of the square and leans up and to the left at 45° (1 px up, 1 px left per unit of height), head up-left of the feet; the top of the head and the shoulders are lit, the front (south) face mid, the right (east) side dark (`docs/GUIDE_25D.md` §1).
3. Palette: `art/palette/uf.hex` only (every index below is verified against the file); aim for ≤ 32 colours per asset; 1-px micro-dither between ramp steps is welcome; no gradients or anti-aliasing against the background.
4. Masters: `art/masters/<layer file>.png` on flat magenta `#FF00FF`, alpha 0/255 after cleaning, no baked ground shadow (the engine draws shadows and the stance square), plus a sidecar `.json` of the same name.
5. Open the reference square named in each brief before drawing (`art/u7_reference_squares/`, `catalog.json` there lists them). They are references and development stand-ins: nothing copied from them ships.
6. Contained: nothing touches the top edge (row 0) or the right edge (column 47); the soles may sit on row 47; the anchor is the bottom centre of the cell, [24, 47], the same pixel in every frame of every layer of a species (AR-600).
7. Sheets (AR-600): one PNG per layer, 4 rows = facings S, W, E, N × 16 columns = stand (1), walk (3), work (3), carry (3), attack (3), cast (2), sleep (1), frames 48×48, so a sheet is 768×192. Each brief describes row 0, column 0 (south stand). W and E are the S and N frames transposed (x and y swapped), never mirrored; N is the back view. Deliver stand and walk first and leave the other columns transparent rather than inventing them; the engine falls back to stand.
8. Sidecar per AR-600 v2: `frameWidth` 48, `frameHeight` 48, `anchor` [24, 47], `facings` ["S", "W", "E", "N"], `animations` { "stand": [0], "walk": [1, 2, 3, 2], "work": [4, 5, 6], "carry": [7, 8, 9], "attack": [10, 11, 12], "cast": [13, 14], "sleep": [15] }, `layer` ("body" or "clothes"), `species`, `stage`, and no `standInSource` (this is original art).
9. Check: `"C:\Program Files\nodejs\node.exe" tools/art_check.js --sidecar art/masters/<layer file>.png` (ignore its 3×-grid check for 48-native masters until the tool is updated; palette, alpha and frame-grid checks must pass).
10. Export = copy the approved file into `game/img/characters/` under its AR-600 name (`$UF_<species>_<gender>_<stage>_body.png`, `$UF_<species>_<gender>_clothes_T<n>.png`) per `docs/ART_STANDARD.md` §5 step 8, with no scaling (masters are already at screen size); then point the catalog's `people.<species>.images` at the body sheet and drop that species' `tint` (the `people` list is one of the catalog lists Gemini may edit, with `objects`, `items.types` and `wildlife.species`; nothing else in `game/data/`).
11. Mark the request row in `docs/ASSET_REQUESTS.md` DELIVERED with the backticked file name (AR-400 for bodies, AR-501 for clothes layers, AR-601 for stage bodies); the user approves each asset before it ships (`art/APPROVALS.md`).
12. Verify a brief you edit with `"C:\Program Files\nodejs\node.exe" tools/check_briefs.js docs/asset_briefs/SEG-11_people.md`; work the briefs in the order below (human first: it is the yardstick for every other species' size).

## Silhouettes inside the square (how the species differ at a glance)
The reference figures are 27×25 (townsman) and 28×30 (ranger) px at 1:1 in the square; every species below stays in that size class, and the differences are in proportion, not in scale. Feet always sit on rows 43–47 at columns 25–38; the crown row and the shoulder line are what change.

| Species | Drawn W×H (man / woman) | Crown row | Shoulder line (man / woman) | What sets it apart |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| human | 28×28 / 26×28 | 20 | 16 px / 13 px | pink skin, brown hair, mid height: the yardstick |
| elf | 25×32 / 24×32 | 16 | 12 px / 11 px | tallest and slimmest, fair hair, pointed ears, a visible neck |
| dwarf | 30×22 / 28×22 | 26 | 19 px / 16 px | short and wide, ruddy skin, red beard (man) or braids (woman), 6-px legs |
| gnome | 20×18 / 18×19 | 30 / 29 | 11 px / 9 px | smallest, the head half the figure, white hair |
| goblin | 31×22 / 29×22 | 26 | 15 px / 13 px | olive skin, hunched (the back higher than the head), long ears, arms to the knees, yellow eyes |
| orc | 34×34 / 31×34 | 14 | 22 px / 19 px | biggest, grey-green skin, tusks, 4-row shoulder mass |
| automaton | 26×30 (one form) | 18 | 14 px | grey metal plates and rods, ball joints, one blue lens, no skin colour at all |

Engine facts every brief below relies on (read from `game/data/UF_WorldCatalog.json` and the plugins on 2026-09-18): clothing tiers are the only visual state the engine has for people today, and only for humans (UF_Colonists `setTier` swaps the whole sheet from the `tiers` list per `gender` in `start` → `pair`: 0 unclad, 1 fiber wraps, 2 hides, 3 tailored; `tiersFor` returns nothing for any other species, which keep one sheet with the catalog's `tint`). The catalog's only clothing items are `fiber_wrap` ("Woven wrap", wear tier 1, recipe "Weave a fiber wrap" from 6 `fiber`) and `hide_cloak` ("Hide cloak", wear tier 2, recipe "Sew a hide cloak" from 2 `hide` and 2 `fiber`, a knife speeds it); no item reaches tier 3. The engine plays `stand` and `walk` only; it draws adults only (AR-601 is REQUESTED, the V40 life cycle is the next build); there is no hurt, dead, corpse or sleeping sprite for people. Age stages are requested by AR-601 for the playable species (human, elf, dwarf, gnome: `playable` true in `factions` → `species`); goblin, orc and automaton are `playable` false, so their stage sheets wait until AR-601 is widened.
