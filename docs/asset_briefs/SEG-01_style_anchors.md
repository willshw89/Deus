# Segment 01: style anchors (written 2026-09-18 by Claude Code)

The four style-lock anchors of `docs/ART_STANDARD.md` §5 step 2: one person, one tree, one wall piece, one ground block. Gemini makes these before anything else, the user approves each one (`art/APPROVALS.md`), and every later segment attaches the approved anchors to its generation requests as the yardstick for size, lean, pixel density and shading. The yardstick for the anchors themselves is the set of reference squares the user approved on 2026-09-18 (`art/review/u7_square_composite.png`, `art/u7_reference_squares/`). Each brief below is the full brief of its asset, the same text as the asset's own segment brief (`anchor_tree` is the oak of SEG-02 word for word; `anchor_ground` is the meadow of SEG-13 with only its cross-references re-pointed; `anchor_person` and `anchor_wall` are the first briefs of the human adult male and the wooden wall, which the people and site-pieces segments copy), plus a "Why this is the anchor" paragraph after its Deliver line. The approved file is the asset's own master under its catalog id; there is no separate anchor file.

## How to work this segment
1. Style: high-resolution 2.5D in the manner of the reference squares, every asset inside one 48×48 screen-pixel square at native resolution (1 art pixel = 1 screen pixel at zoom 1). No 16×16 upscaling: the 16×16 masters in `art/masters/` (settler_male_16x16, oak, wall_wood) and their 3× copies are superseded. `docs/ART_STANDARD.md` is binding.
2. Projection: height leans up and to the left at 45° (1 px up and 1 px left per unit of height); the top, south and east faces are visible; the base sits in the bottom-right region of the square (`docs/GUIDE_25D.md`). Ground is flat, seen from straight above.
3. Palette: `art/palette/uf.hex` only; every index in these briefs is verified against the file (index, then hex). Aim for 32 colours or fewer per asset; 1-px micro-dither between ramp steps is welcome; no gradients, no anti-aliasing against the background.
4. Masters: `art/masters/<id>.png` on flat magenta `#FF00FF` (ground blocks have no background: every pixel painted), alpha 0 or 255 after cleaning, no baked ground shadow (the engine draws shadows).
5. Contained: nothing touches the top edge (row 0) or the right edge (column 47); the anchor of anything that stands on a cell is the bottom centre of the cell, [24, 47]. Wall pieces and ground tiles fill their cell instead.
6. Sidecar: `art/masters/<id>.json` per AR-600 (`frameWidth`, `frameHeight`, `anchor`, `facings`, `animations`; for people also `layer`, `species`, `stage`; a ground block has anchor null, facings [], animations {}, layer "ground" and its slot, as SEG-13 says).
7. Open the reference square named in the brief before drawing (`art/u7_reference_squares/<key>_48.png`; the `_48_4x.png` copy shows the pixels). It is a reference and a development stand-in: nothing copied from it ships.
8. Order: anchor_person, anchor_tree, anchor_wall, anchor_ground. Stop after each one for the user's approval; the next segment starts only when all four are approved.
9. Check: `"C:\Program Files\nodejs\node.exe" tools/art_check.js --sidecar art/masters/<id>.png` (`--type tileset` instead of `--sidecar` for the ground block; ignore the tool's 3×-grid check for 48-native masters until it is updated).
10. Export: copy the approved master into `game/img/` per `docs/ART_STANDARD.md` §5 step 8 with no scaling (masters are already at screen size): `characters/` for the person and the tree under the name the catalog's `image` field uses; the wall set and the ground block go into `game/img/tilesets/` through Claude Code, who wires the tiles (SEG-13 line 9).
11. Then mark the request row in `docs/ASSET_REQUESTS.md` DELIVERED with the backticked file name (AR-400, AR-102 with the older AR-021, AR-104, AR-100). No text, borders or lore in file names or images.
12. Verify an edited brief with `"C:\Program Files\nodejs\node.exe" tools/check_briefs.js docs/asset_briefs/SEG-01_style_anchors.md` and fix every FAIL.

### anchor_person — Human, adult male (AR-400)
- **Category**: People
- **Dimensions**: fits inside one 48×48 screen-pixel square at native resolution (1 art pixel = 1 screen pixel at zoom 1); the drawn figure is 29 px wide and 36 px tall (the head 12 px of that)
- **Anchor**: bottom-centre of the cell `[24, 47]` (the engine draws every sprite with its anchor at the cell's bottom centre; the feet straddle it)
- **Projection**: U7 2.5D oblique: the figure stands on its feet in the bottom-right region and leans up and to the left at 45° (1 px up and 1 px left per unit of height), so the shoulders sit 17 px left of the feet; the top of the head and the left shoulder are lit, the front (south) is mid, the right (east) side of every limb is dark
- **Reference**: `art/u7_reference_squares/u7_townsman_south_48.png` (SHAPES.VGA shape 265, frame 16, at 1:1; the `$U7_Townsman` stand-in) and shape 458 (the `$Adam` tier-0 stand-in in STATUS → Stand-ins; the square labelled 458 in the approved composite); reference only, nothing copied ships

Why this is the anchor: people are most of what moves on the map, and every other figure (the woman, the six other species, the age stages, the clothing and tool layers, every creature) must match this frame's lean, pixel density and shading before it is drawn, so the user approves it first (`docs/ART_STANDARD.md` §5 step 2). It replaces the 16×16 settler master `art/masters/settler_male_16x16.png` and its 3× copy `settler_male_48x48.png`, both superseded. Once approved, attach this frame to every later generation request as the size and style yardstick.

#### Readability Check:

Deliver: art/masters/oak.png (48×48, magenta background) + art/masters/oak.json (frameWidth 48, frameHeight 48, anchor [24, 47], facings ["S"], animations { "stand": [0] }).

Why this is the anchor: the text above is SEG-02's oak brief unchanged, so "this segment" and "next brief" in it mean SEG-02, where the stump is the last brief and the birch the second. It replaces the 16×16 master `art/masters/oak.png` and the 3× copy `oak_tree_native_48.png`, both superseded. Attach the approved oak to every later plant and rock request.

### anchor_wall — Wooden wall (AR-104)
- **Category**: Building
- **Dimensions**: a set of 48×48 pieces on one sheet (the engine assembles them like RPG Maker chipset walls, the same set and order as `wall_stone`); the anchor is the straight east–west piece, which fills its square: 48 px wide and 48 px tall
- **Anchor**: none (tile); each piece fills its cell
- **Projection**: U7 2.5D oblique: the top face (the ring of log ends) is a lit band along the top of the cell, the south face (the log fronts) fills the rest, and the east face shows on an east end piece as a dark 6-px strip; in a continuous run the up-left lean of each piece is absorbed by its neighbours, so only the log ends' 1-px left offset and the end pieces show it
- **Reference**: none in `art/u7_reference_squares/`; nearest are `u7_stump_48.png` (SHAPES.VGA shape 313) for weathered bark and end grain and `u7_boulder_48.png` (shape 342) for how a solid block's three faces meet (reference only, nothing copied ships); the stock `Outside_B` tile 80 in use today is the placeholder, not a reference

### anchor_ground — Meadow (AR-100)
- **Category**: Ground (A2 autotile)
- **Dimensions**: an RPG Maker A2 autotile block of 2×3 tiles of 48×48 = 96×144 px at native resolution (1 art pixel = 1 screen pixel at zoom 1): one seamless 48×48 base tile repeated 2 across and 3 down, with the edge ring and notches painted over it
- **Anchor**: none (tile)
- **Projection**: flat (ground tile): seen from straight above, no lean and no faces; the U7 2.5D objects and units draw on top of it, so the ground stays calm and micro-textured
- **Reference**: `game/img/tilesets/U7_Ground_A2.png`, the grass block at columns 0–95, rows 0–143 (SHAPES.VGA flat ground shape 4 decoded at 3×; its five colours are palette 168–172; reference only, nothing copied ships), and `art/review/u7_square_composite.png` for the objects that will stand on it
- **Palette Ramps**: Grass 168 `#71864D` (sunlit blade tips), 169 `#5D7139` (lit blades), 170 `#4D5D28` (field), 171 `#415120` (shade between blades), 172 `#39451C` (deepest pockets); Dry tip 202 `#7D9600` (four pixels at most); Ring 173 `#313D18`; no background colour: every pixel of a ground block is opaque.

#### Primary State Visual Description:
On the 48×48 base tile (rows and columns 0–47) the field is 170 broken by 171 in an irregular 1-px micro-dither that covers about 40% of the tile, with no run of either colour longer than 4 px. Over it stand 24 to 28 short blades, each 1 px wide and 3 to 5 px tall and drawn straight up because the ground is flat, in 169 with a single 168 pixel at the top of each; no two blades touch, 6 to 8 single pixels of 172 sit in the pockets beneath them, and at most four blade tips are 202. The tile wraps: a blade cut by column 47 continues at column 0, one cut by row 47 continues at row 0, and the dither joins across both edges, so the tile lays 2 across and 3 down without a seam. In the 96×144 block the showcase tile (columns 0–47, rows 0–47) carries a 2-px ring of 173 on rows 0–1, rows 46–47, columns 0–1 and columns 46–47; the inner-corner tile (columns 48–95, rows 0–47) is the plain texture with an L-shaped notch of 173, 2 px thick and 6 px long along both edges, at each of its four corners (48,0), (95,0), (48,47) and (95,47); the lower square (columns 0–95, rows 48–143) has the same 2-px ring of 173 on its outer border only (rows 48–49, rows 142–143, columns 0–1, columns 94–95) and nothing on its internal seams at column 48 and row 96. Nothing else differs between the six tiles, there is no baked shadow or lit side, and no pixel is transparent.

#### Interaction / Transformed State Description:
"dig" (a right-click job in the engine, work 80, dropping a `stone` item 1 time in 4) turns the cell into `dirt`, which has its own brief and file in the ground tiles b segment (SEG-14); the engine then recomputes the autotile shapes of the cell and its eight neighbours from these blocks. At generation, cursed regions lay `cursed_grass` and blessed regions `blessed_grass` in place of meadow (their own briefs, SEG-14); that is a swap when the map is made, not a change in play. There is no other second state in the engine.

#### Readability Check:
At zoom ⅓ the meadow is the calm olive-green field, duller and greyer than the saturated lush grass, greener than the golden dry grass and without the lush grass's yellow flecks; its 173 ring shows only where a biome ends.

Deliver: art/masters/meadow.png (96×144 A2 block, every pixel opaque, so no magenta is left) + art/masters/meadow.json (frameWidth 48, frameHeight 48, anchor null, facings [], animations {}, layer "ground", slot 0). Slot 0 = column 0, row 0 of the assembled A2 sheet (SEG-13, "How to work this segment", line 9).

Why this is the anchor: the text above is SEG-13's meadow brief with its cross-references pointed at SEG-13 and SEG-14. Ground is under everything: the person, the tree and the wall are judged on it in the review page (`docs/ART_STANDARD.md` §5 step 6), so its value and grain decide how much contrast every other asset needs, and the 21 other ground kinds of AR-100 copy its blade construction, fleck density and ring rule with their own ramps. It replaces the code-drawn placeholder (kind 0 of the generated A2 sheet) and the three-kind stand-in sheet. Attach the approved block to every later ground and plant request.
