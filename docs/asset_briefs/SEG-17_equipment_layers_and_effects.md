# Segment 17: equipment layers and effects (written 2026-09-18 by Claude Code)

The layers that ride on a person's body sheet under the layered character standard (AR-600 in `docs/ASSET_REQUESTS.md`): the three held tools the catalog has (stone axe, stone knife, stone pick), a wooden shield and a leather cap for the two equipment slots of the design that have no item yet (VISION V49 slots: helmet, weapon, shield, torso, legs), the clothing tiers 1–3 split into torso and legs, the casting glow with its burst sprite, and two pose contracts (the work cycle and the attack cycle) that fix where the body's hands are in the frames every layer must register on. Every fact below about slots, tiers, job types, tool multipliers and what the engine draws today comes from `game/data/UF_WorldCatalog.json` (`items.types`, `recipes.list`, `start.pair`, `wildlife.species`, `colony.plan`), `docs/ASSET_INVENTORY.md` and the plugins (`UF_Colonists.js`, `UF_Jobs.js`), read 2026-09-18. The ground-item briefs of the same tools and clothes are in SEG-07 and are only cross-referenced here.

## How to work this segment
1. Style: high-resolution 2.5D in the manner of the reference squares, every frame inside one 48×48 screen-pixel square at native resolution (1 art pixel = 1 screen pixel at zoom 1). No 16×16 upscaling. `docs/ART_STANDARD.md` is binding.
2. Projection: height leans up and to the left at 45° (1 px up, 1 px left per unit of height); the top, south and east faces are visible (`docs/GUIDE_25D.md`). A layer follows the lean of the body it rides on: a haft held upright leans up-left, a raised hand moves up-left, a blade pointed at the ground runs down-right, and anything lying flat on the ground has no lean at all.
3. Layers ride on the body: every layer here is a sheet on the same 48×48 frame grid and the same anchor [24, 47] as the body sheet (AR-600), drawn over it frame by frame. The body quoted throughout is the anchor person of SEG-01 (`anchor_person`, the human adult male: feet on rows 45–47 at columns 25–29 and 31–35, hip wrap on rows 35–39 at columns 16–27, shoulder line on row 26 at columns 7–22, viewer's-left hand at columns 13–15 on rows 36–38, viewer's-right hand at columns 27–29 on rows 36–38, head 10×12 on rows 12–23 at columns 7–16). Draw each layer over that body frame on a scratch layer, then export the layer alone. The woman and the other species get the same layers fitted to their body briefs in the people segment.
4. Columns are AR-600's, and only those: stand (0), walk (1–3), work (4–6), carry (7–9), attack (10–12), cast (13–14), sleep (15): 16 columns of 48 px = 768 px wide, 4 rows (S, W, E, N) = 192 px tall. If a body brief adds hurt or dead columns after column 15, its layers gain the same columns, transparent where the item is not shown. W and E are transposed per GUIDE_25D (never mirrored), so keep column 0 and row 0 empty in every frame: a transposed frame then stays contained too.
5. Palette: `art/palette/uf.hex` only (every index below is verified against the file); aim for 32 colours or fewer per asset; 1-px micro-dither between ramp steps is welcome, and a dithered edge against transparency is the only soft edge allowed (alpha is 0 or 255); no gradients, no anti-aliasing against the background.
6. Masters: `art/masters/<id>.png` on flat magenta `#FF00FF`, alpha 0/255 after cleaning, no baked ground shadow (the engine draws shadows); a layer is transparent wherever the item is not shown. Where the body's hand grips a tool, leave the hand's 3×3 pixels transparent so the body's hand reads in front of the haft.
7. Reference: open the reference square named in each brief before drawing (`art/u7_reference_squares/<key>_48.png`; the `_48_4x.png` copy shows the pixels). There is no tool, shield, helmet or effect square: the person squares give the hand's place, and the ground-item briefs of SEG-07 give each tool's shape and colours. References and development stand-ins only: nothing copied from them ships.
8. Sidecar: `art/masters/<id>.json` per AR-600 v2 (`frameWidth` 48, `frameHeight` 48, `anchor` [24, 47], `facings`, `animations` with AR-600's frame indices, `layer` "held", "shield", "clothes" or "fx" (the helmet: see its brief), plus `species` and `stage` for clothes).
9. Order: eq_weapon_stone_axe, eq_weapon_stone_knife, eq_weapon_stone_pick, eq_shield_wood, eq_helmet_leather, eq_torso_tiers, eq_legs_tiers, eq_work_animations, eq_attack_animations, eq_cast_effect. The two animation briefs are pose contracts for the body's work and attack columns, so read them before drawing any layer's columns 4–6 and 10–12, and draw their proof sheets before the full layer sheets.
10. Check: `"C:\Program Files\nodejs\node.exe" tools/art_check.js --sidecar art/masters/<id>.png` (ignore its 3×-grid check for 48-native masters until the tool is updated); then compose the layer over the body frame at 1× and 4× in `art/review/` and look at every frame: the same item in every frame, in the hand, contained.
11. Export: copy the approved master into `game/img/characters/` per `docs/ART_STANDARD.md` §5 step 8 with no scaling, under the AR-600 layer name each Deliver line gives (`$UF_held_<item>.png`, `$UF_shield_<kind>.png`, `$UF_human_<gender>_clothes_T<n>.png`, `$UF_fx_<effect>.png`). Then mark the request row in `docs/ASSET_REQUESTS.md` (AR-600, or AR-501 for the clothing tiers) DELIVERED with the backticked file name; the user approves each asset before it ships (`art/APPROVALS.md`).
12. Verify a brief you edit with `"C:\Program Files\nodejs\node.exe" tools/check_briefs.js docs/asset_briefs/SEG-17_equipment_layers_and_effects.md` and fix every FAIL. Where an engine state does not exist yet, the brief says so in its Interaction section, and Gemini draws the frames without waiting for the engine.
